## Three words that matter
The writer of Hebrews tells a group of believers they should be further along by now. Then verse 14 turns: solid food is for the mature, "who by constant use have trained themselves to distinguish good from evil." DeWayne keys on "constant use." Discernment is not something you know. It's something you build, the way an athlete builds strength.

## Reps, not days
The old rule says a habit takes 21 days. The truth is that repetition builds it, not the calendar. Three reps a day can lock something in within ten days, and once every other day might take three months. Reading scripture without doing anything with it feels productive, but it's motion. Doing what it says is action, and action is the rep that counts.

## Why it matters
You can sit in church for ten years and still not be ready for the heavier things, the same way a second grader isn't ready for trigonometry. And the growth isn't only for you. Your spouse, your kids, your coworkers, and the barista who hands you your coffee all feel the difference.

## A two-question check
Someone on the call shared a simple habit. Every time you open the Bible, ask:
- Lord, what do you want me to know?
- Lord, what do you want me to do with it?
