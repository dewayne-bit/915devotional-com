## Not Captain America's shield
The shield Paul pictured was roughly two feet by four feet, wood wrapped in leather. Soldiers locked them side by side and overhead, since arrows fly in an arc.

## Arrows meant to scatter
Arrows were dipped in pitch and lit. The goal wasn't one lucky kill. It was panic: if your gear catches fire, you drop it and run, and the wall breaks. The enemy's darts work the same way. "You're too dirty. God won't forgive you." They don't kill you. They drive you away.

## Soaked in water
Soldiers soaked their shields so arrows went out on contact. DeWayne connects that to Jesus' living water. One participant added that wet leather tightens as it dries, making the shield stronger. DeWayne's take: every Bible study and church service soaks and strengthens your faith.

## In addition, or above all
Some translations say "above all." DeWayne says the Greek can mean either one, and either way faith protects everything else. Another participant shared Hebrews 11:1: faith is the substance of things hoped for.
